Overview

Once the weather is taken out, Lucknow's winters are not getting cleaner.

Winter smog depends on wind, rain and how deep the layer of mixing air is. This dashboard asks what is left of six years of PM2.5 readings at Lalbagh after a model has accounted for the weather.

Winter average, raw and weather-adjusted

December to February, µg/m³. Adjusted means the same winter replayed under typical weather for each month and hour.

2021-22 looked like a big improvement in the raw numbers (97). It was a well-ventilated winter. Adjusted, it sits at 105, and the next two winters are 117 and 108 before 2024-25 rises to 126.

Each year, 1 January to 19 October

A like-for-like comparison. The last months of 2025 are left out because the sensor was unreliable.

2025 comes out higher than 2024 both raw (68 against 61) and adjusted (72 against 58). Adjusting widens the gap: 2024's weather was a little worse than typical and 2025's a little better.

What happened during the 2020 lockdown?

The model was trained without the lockdown weeks, then asked what 25 March to 31 May should have looked like. Every other year gets the same test, to show how wrong the method is when nothing unusual happens.

Weather

Trapped air is dirtier air.

When the layer of air near the ground is shallow, the same emissions mix into less volume. The model uses this, and the other weather it can see, to predict PM2.5 without knowing anything about traffic or burning.

PM2.5 by boundary layer height

November to January hours, split into eight equal groups by boundary layer height. µg/m³.

What the model leans on

How much accuracy is lost when one input is shuffled. Higher means the model needs it more.

Long-term trend is the model's stand-in for everything that changed across years and is not weather, which includes emissions. Rain over the last three days, the hour of day and boundary layer height come next.

Daily means. The model never saw the days it is predicting here: whole two-week blocks were held out during training.

Stations

From 20 October 2025, Lalbagh's PM2.5 stops behaving like PM2.5.

Fine particles (PM2.5) are a part of coarse ones (PM10), so the two should rise and fall together in a steady proportion. Lalbagh's proportion fell and its correlation weakened, while a second station showed no such change.

PM2.5 per unit of PM10, by month

Slope of daily PM2.5 on PM10. The green band is the range of a working instrument. The red region is excluded from every other chart on this site.

How closely they move together

Correlation of daily PM2.5 with PM10, by month. 1 means perfect agreement.

November and December, 2024 against 2025

Daily PM2.5 and PM10 averaged over the two months, µg/m³.

Coarse particles agree at both stations. Fine particles do not: Lalbagh's PM2.5 fell more than twice as much as the other station's.

What this proves, and what it does not. It is strong circumstantial evidence of an instrument fault, not a confirmed one. Only the station operator can confirm that. The second station is also an imperfect referee: its PM2.5 and Lalbagh's agreed poorly in parts of 2025. The strongest evidence is Lalbagh's PM2.5 stopping following Lalbagh's own PM10. Other explanations, such as a real change in local emissions, cannot be ruled out from public data alone.
Simulator

Change the weather and see what the model expects.

A deliberately simple model that knows only the month, the hour and five weather readings. It shows how much of the air quality weather can explain. It is not a forecast.

Data

Hourly pollution from the Central Pollution Control Board's CCR portal (Lalbagh, site 277; B R Ambedkar University, site 5460). Weather from ERA5 reanalysis, Copernicus Climate Change Service (Hersbach et al., 2023, DOI 10.24381/cds.adbb2d47).

Limits

One monitoring station. ERA5 is a roughly 28 km grid cell, not a measurement at the station. CPCB data is provisional. Category colours follow CPCB bands for 24-hour averages. 1,677 hours of Lalbagh PM2.5 from 20 October 2025 are excluded.
